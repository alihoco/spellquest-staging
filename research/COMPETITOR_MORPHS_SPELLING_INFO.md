# SpellQuest competitor and methodology study — Morphs / spelling.info
**Reviewed:** 11 November 2026  
**Status:** Public-source functional and pedagogical review; no authenticated gameplay test and no independent outcome evaluation  
**Product:** Morphs, by Grammarsaurus Ltd  
**Pupil access:** https://spelling.info/  
**Developer's description:** https://grammarsaurus.co.uk/portal/morphs/

## What could be verified

- The `spelling.info` home page is a JavaScript-heavy application that did not provide readable content through the available inspection. The substantive information here comes from the official Grammarsaurus product pages, published screenshots, the developer/designer's portfolio, FAQs, and independent spelling guidance; **actual pupil-login workflows have not been exercised**.
- The premise is a microscopic adventure world called the Colony: gather resources, build a base, customise a character and face bugs. Learning actions power gameplay.
- Each level addresses a specific **morpheme**, with a short tutorial and later application in gameplay. The scheme is systematic, structured and cumulative and uses bases, prefixes, suffixes and suffixing rules.
- The game reports errors/strengths through a teacher-facing spelling heatmap and links to targeted materials.
- Public screenshots show three-choice correct-spelling selection and a level-completion screen, but they do **not** verify all task types, narration coverage, phoneme accuracy, pacing, adaptations or accessibility in operation.
- The game's stated alignment is strongest for school-led spelling teaching (roughly primary-school ages); public sources differ on the number of islands/levels and app age ratings, so avoid claiming a fixed count without a live check.
- A Grammarsaurus subscription is generally required for gameplay; the App Store download is not the same as free unfettered access.

## What we should learn from Morphs

| Observed approach | Why it may be valuable | SpellQuest adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| One morpheme per lesson | Meaningful word components and reuse across related words; avoids random disconnected lists | Organise advanced instruction by meaningful bases/prefixes/suffixes; early phonology and sound–grapheme work remain separate foundations |
| Explicit tutorial followed by application | The teaching stage has a defined purpose before game practice | Always demonstrate/teach, provide supported practice, then check unaided spelling on a later date |
| Adventure-world progression | Motivation and return visits | Age-appropriate worlds and rewards for personal progress, not winning over others |
| Three-choice spelling discrimination (visible screenshot) | Accessible low-demand recognition and error spotting | Use as *supported recognition*; do not confuse a 1-in-3 recognition success with autonomous recall |
| Teacher heatmap | Granular diagnosis of recurring mistakes | Private child growth map with parent/teacher detail, and targeted feedback by phoneme–grapheme pattern, orthography and morphology |
| Word families and suffixing patterns | Potential for transfer beyond memorising one item | Practise new/untaught words sharing a target morpheme, including independent spelling in meaningful contexts |

## Critical cautions for dyslexia-specific design

1. **Morphology is valuable but not sufficient alone.** Many dyslexic learners also need explicit phonological awareness, phoneme–grapheme teaching and orthographic pattern instruction. Preserve phonics and meaning together; do not label arbitrary spelling chunks as spoken sounds.
2. **Do not infer dyslexia-specific efficacy from product messaging.** The Morphs marketing description says it is inclusive/effective for SEND and literacy difficulties. Independent controlled outcome evidence *for this particular game* was not identified in this inspection. Evidence supporting morphological instruction as a category is not evidence proving Morphs itself improves retained spelling.
3. **Recognition is not production.** Showing three spellings can teach distinctions but provides assistance and guessing opportunities; report that separately from independent spelling with an accessible full alphabet or physical keyboard.
4. **Game progress is not retained mastery.** Avoid relying on levels, resources, completion percentages or boss victories as primary learning metrics. Test independent recall after several days/weeks, and transfer to untaught related words.
5. **Audio-first accessibility remains unverified for Morphs.** Public sources describe word audio integration but do not establish that all instructions are narrated, that individual sounds are accurate, or that the user can operate every part without reading. SpellQuest should explicitly address this, with educator-checked audio and user-paced replay.
6. **Age-respectful UX matters.** Cartoon adventure aesthetics may appeal to younger learners; it should not be the only option for teens and adults learning the same fundamentals.
7. **Avoid punitive pressure.** Compare each learner only with their own progress. Avoid public leaderboards, speed-based scores, peer rankings or age-level shaming.
8. **Question the data interpretation.** A 'heatmap' is only as useful as its measurement model. Separate assisted answers, unaided correct spellings, long-term retention and motor/input errors. Ensure safe storage and consent for minors.

## Specific original SpellQuest concepts to pilot

- **Morpheme explorer:** inspect a real base and genuine prefixes/suffixes; explain what each means; build valid related words; hear the pronunciation change where applicable.
- **Word detective:** decide which spelling pattern fits a *spoken* target; use targeted distractors only after explicit teaching.
- **Repair the bridge:** given a word initially misspelled by the learner, review its sound–spelling mapping and one meaningful spelling rule; build/retrieve again.
- **Personal progress map:** visualise the child's own retained mastery of morphemes and spelling patterns over months, without normative ranking.
- **Multi-modal access:** letters/grapheme tiles, three-choice recognition and full on-screen alphabet, with assistance level tracked separately.
- **Audio-guided navigation:** spoken task instructions in the learner's selected voice, but not inaccurate synthetic pseudo-phonemes.
- **Untaught-word missions:** practise transfer from learned morphemes to new words that share a real linguistic structure.

## Source and evidence distinction

- *Observed product features* — marketing descriptions, screenshots and agency case study; these are credible for describing intended features, but not independent efficacy.
- *Research-backed teaching mechanisms* — International Dyslexia Association spelling guidance and relevant morphology/phonics evidence; applicable to pedagogy, not a controlled evaluation of Morphs.
- *Untested hypotheses* — whether a progression game improves motivation, whether Morphs' specific activities outperform alternatives, or whether a given feature is more effective for dyslexic learners.

**Conclusion:** Learn from the explicit morphology progression, engagement layer and diagnostic feedback; improve by building a verified sound–spelling foundation, safe audio-first accessibility, age-respectful choices, and rigorous separate measurement of unaided delayed spelling. Do not copy original artwork, characters, stories, written tutorials, proprietary lesson contents or branded game mechanics.
