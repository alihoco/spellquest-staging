# SpellQuest Longitudinal Research Programme — UK 12-month Concept v1.0

**Status:** Proposed research architecture, not an approved or commenced study.
**Date:** 11 November 2026
**Recruitment ambition:** 5,000–10,000 learners, school-aged and adult, across the UK over one year.
**Principle:** Learning comes first. Joining the educational service must not require participation in additional research.

## Objectives
1. Estimate how much independent spelling improves and is retained under an evidence-informed adaptive tutoring system.
2. Compare structured literacy core (A), structured literacy plus original meaning-and-making activity (B), and structured literacy plus extra time-matched practice (C).
3. Separate genuine incremental method benefits from practice time, easier word sets, changing motivation, school instruction and regression to the mean.
4. Identify where results are consistent across word types, learning histories and ages; avoid unsupported permanent learner-style labels.

## Research population and recruitment
- Learners aged 4–16 and adults; separate analysis of school samples, self-selected online sample, and adults.
- Record dyslexia status only if needed and lawfully justified: documented identification / self-report / not recorded; do not use spell-test results as a diagnosis.
- Recruit through school partnerships and families, avoiding class-wide pressure or exclusion.
- Document geographic, socio-economic and access variation, with minimal justified data collection.
- Plan for attrition, missing assessments and school-level clustering. Do not equate 10,000 sign-ups with 10,000 evaluable participants.
- Predefine sample sizes and statistical power with research collaborators, taking realistic follow-up rates and cluster effects into account.

## Stages
**Stage 0, prelaunch:** Independent specialist review of curriculum, phonemes, syllables, spelling patterns, audio, child privacy and accessibility. Pilot the teaching software without formal efficacy claims.

**Stage 1, feasibility:** Modest opt-in pilot to establish consent flow, acceptability, audio reliability, missing data, ability to run delayed word tests and word-set matching.

**Stage 2, formal preregistered evaluation:** Recruit varied learners; baseline spelling and spelling pattern competence measured independently of age. Randomise matched word blocks to A/B/C conditions where ethically appropriate. Maintain common foundational instruction for every arm; only optional supplementary activities differ.

**Stage 3, longitudinal follow-up:** Measure immediate independent spelling; independent delayed recall at 7 and 28 days (suggested, not proven optimal); broader checkpoints across 3, 6 and 12 months. Include transfer to novel related words. Track actual exposure time, supports shown and practice attempts.

**Stage 4, analysis and reporting:** Hierarchical learner- and school-aware models, missingness analysis, confidence intervals and preregistered primary/secondary outcomes. Report null and adverse findings. Publish aggregate findings; share truly anonymised data only where disclosure risk is carefully assessed and research permissions cover reuse.

## Primary and secondary outcomes
**Primary:** Delayed independent spelling accuracy under comparable no-target-clue conditions, with accessible full alphabet or keyboard response options.

**Secondary:** Time-normalised learning gain, maintenance at 28 days and later, new-word transfer, child comfort/confidence, continued engagement, and usefulness of different input methods.

Do not count choosing between spellings or assembling from exactly the supplied letters as independent mastery.

## Privacy, safeguarding and ethics gates
- UK ICO Children's Code and UK GDPR: high privacy by default, data minimisation, clear child-facing notices, a DPIA, safeguarding, retention and deletion processes, lawful basis and assessment of profiling risks.
- Distinguish service-level adaptation from optional research processing; do not bundle research opt-in into registration.
- BERA 2024 guidance: voluntary informed consent, child assent suitable to age and reading abilities, adult guardian involvement where appropriate, withdrawal without losing access to learning.
- Independent research ethics review before formal experiments involving children; school agreements and data processing agreements where applicable.
- Use pseudonymous IDs, strict access controls, no public child records, no raw microphone recordings by default.
- If adaptive experimentation has possible detrimental consequences or a child is struggling, preserve a proven baseline, provide ordinary help immediately and allow opt-out without penalty.
- Any claimed 'largest study' or proven 'best method' must be independently substantiated; do not use it as marketing before results exist.

## Analysis details and quality assurance
- Pre-register the causal contrast B versus C (time-matched) as central to distinguishing technique effects from greater exposure.
- Use counterbalanced matched word sets; account for word difficulty, baseline familiarity and carryover, and randomization at appropriate unit.
- Keep separate: learner engagement analytics, educational progress for tutoring, and formally consented research data.
- Minimum report fields: number invited, enrolled, consented, active, assessed, followed up; intervention minutes; baseline balance; attrition by arm; aggregate retention; transfer; confidence interval; safety and accessibility outcomes.
- Independent research partner and qualified statistician to review randomization, estimands, power, clustering and missing data before enrollment.

## Published research outputs (proposed)
1. Public transparent protocol and outcome definitions.
2. Accessible annual report of aggregate learning gains with limitations.
3. Educational practitioner summary for teachers and SENCOs.
4. Methodology paper and independently reviewed journal submission, including null findings.
5. Optional privacy-protected de-identified research dataset if technically, ethically and legally feasible.
