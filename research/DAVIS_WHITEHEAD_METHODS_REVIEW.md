# SpellQuest research review — Davis methods and Richard Whitehead

**Reviewed:** 11 November 2026  
**Scope:** Davis dyslexia programmes, Richard Whitehead's contributions, visual/experiential learning and possible extensions to SpellQuest's spelling pedagogy  
**Evidence position:** Exploratory hybrid *design*, not endorsement or proof that Davis theories or the Davis programme outperform structured literacy

## 1. Identity, origins and scope

The person on the Davis Learning Foundation team page is **Richard Whitehead** (not Whiteley). He is described as director, workshop presenter, Davis Specialist, former classroom educator, Patoss-accredited Specialist Teacher Assessor and author of *Why Tyrannosaurus But Not If?*. His work elaborates and teaches principles developed by **Ronald D. Davis**. Do not attribute Davis methodology exclusively to Whitehead or frame Whitehead's book as a controlled efficacy trial.

- Team page: https://www.davislearningfoundation.org.uk/our-team
- Davis profile: https://davismethod.com/facilitator/richard-whitehead/
- Book: https://www.davismethod.co.uk/product-page/richard-whitehead-why-tyrannosaurus-but-not-if

## 2. The method, described by its proponents

Davis practitioners promote learner strengths, visual-spatial imagination, active participation and concept mastery. Their programme uses clay models for alphabets, symbols and word meaning; orientation/attention tools; and pronunciation and reading exercises. A recurring theme is connecting **written appearance, spoken form and meaning** into a coherent experience. Whitehead particularly emphasises making difficult abstract concepts meaningful and concrete.

The approach differs substantially from conventional systematic phonics in its explanation of dyslexia (e.g. a prominent role for disorientation) and in promoting deep, self-directed mastery instead of drill. We must **not** assume every dyslexic person is primarily a visual thinker or that dyslexia is caused by disorientation.

## 3. Evidence audit — distinguish claims, findings and confidence

### 3.1 Dyslexia association / structured-literacy baseline

The International Dyslexia Association describes explicit, systematic, cumulative and responsive teaching of phoneme–grapheme connections, orthographic conventions, syllables/stress and morphology as a strong basis for reading and spelling instruction. Practice, informative feedback and gradual automation are integral. This remains SpellQuest's **primary evidence-informed instructional foundation**.

- https://dyslexiaida.org/structured-literacy-effective-instruction-for-students-with-dyslexia-and-related-reading-difficulties/
- https://dyslexiaida.org/spelling/

### 3.2 Studies specifically involving Davis-based instruction

- **van Staden, Tolmie & Badenhorst (2009)**, *Africa Education Review*, DOI 10.1080/18146620903274605: controlled comparison of approximately 18 Afrikaans-speaking intermediate dyslexic learners per group across nine months. A Davis-based multimodal intervention was associated with higher reading/spelling outcomes than a comparison receiving other remedial teaching. **Interpretation:** signals possible value and warrants replication, but small sample, specialised language/school context, multi-component package and comparator differences preclude general superiority claims or isolating clay/visualisation effects.
  - https://www.dyslexia.com/reference/van-staden-2009/
  - https://scholar.ufs.ac.za/items/1dc3cbb0-f78b-4327-8094-22a1b936d7bc
- The Davis Association's own evidence compilation lists additional small studies, case studies, matched-group work, unpublished/provider before-after series and dissertations. This means the claim that Davis approaches have **no studies at all** is false; it does **not** mean they have robust independent replication for the full dyslexia programme, or for delayed English spelling.
  - https://www.dyslexia.com/research/articles/evidence-base-methods/
  - https://www.dyslexia.com/referencecontent/original-research/davis-methods/
- A critical review argues that Davis claims about moving a 'mind's eye' or disorientation causing dyslexia lack adequate direct empirical validation, and that many intervention reports are small or do not test generalisation and robust comparators.
  - https://www.dyslexicadvantage.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Review-of-Studies-to-Remediate-LDs.pdf
- A University of Michigan dyslexia education commentary was sceptical about programme outcome evidence as reviewed at its publication time; interpret as a dated expert opinion, **not** a comprehensive recent systematic review establishing no evidence.
  - https://dyslexiahelp.umich.edu/ask-dr-pierson/deciphering-between-evidence-based-strategies/

### 3.3 Important tension: 'No drill or repetition'

Davis proponents describe mastery from a rich, creative experience and criticise repetitive drill. This highlights a valuable design goal: **avoid mindless copying and monotonous practice**. However, it should not displace strategically spaced *retrieval practice*, delayed independent spelling and cumulative review, which are central to our measurable learning objective. No source here establishes that one rich exposure produces permanent mastery across dyslexic learners.

### 3.4 Evidence conclusions

**More substantiated:** Explicit teaching of sound–grapheme, orthographic and morphological structure, active feedback, cumulative practice; engaged, concrete hands-on exercises are compatible with structured instruction.

**Promising but unproven as superior for spelling:** 3D/visual modelling, making abstract words concrete through imagery, learner-generated associations, self-directed concept exploration, tactile interactions, imagery-based word meaning and lowered anxiety.

**Do not adopt as facts:** A uniform visual-spatial dyslexic learning style; disorientation as a universal explanation for dyslexia; the claim that a Davis method permanently corrects dyslexia; the idea that repetition and phonics can be safely eliminated; voice or image generation validating pronunciation without linguistic review.

## 4. Side-by-side methodology comparison

| Dimension | Structured Literacy / IDA baseline | Davis tradition | SpellQuest decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fundamental spelling knowledge | Explicit phonemes, graphemes, conventions, syllables/stress, morphemes | Visual/experiential connection among word appearance, sound and meaning | Keep explicit linguistic analysis central; add meaning-based, active learning |
| Word meaning | Morphology and vocabulary integrated | Strong emphasis on concrete meaning, including abstract 'trigger' words | Try child-generated meanings and concrete scenes as optional reinforcement |
| Learning activity | Modelling, guided practice, feedback and cumulative retrieval | Clay modelling, symbol mastery and personalised discovery | Offer age-respectful interactive 2D/3D word-building that reinforces *verified* patterns |
| Focus and confidence | Responsive teaching and explicit success | Learner strengths, self-management and reduced pressure | Use safe, optional check-in/focus routines, no unsupported therapeutic claims |
| Review | Practice and delayed retrieval | 'No drill' ethos; lasting mastery from meaningful learning | No rote copying; retain well-spaced independent retrieval |
| Differentiation | Diagnostic and responsive to performance | Tailored to individuals and perceived strengths | Adapt to demonstrated benefit; no permanent visual/auditory learning-style labels |
| Evidence | Stronger broad research foundation | Mixed, limited programme-level evidence | Experimental add-ons evaluated for delayed unaided English spelling |

## 5. Proposed optional 'Meaning and Making' branch in SpellQuest

**Trigger:** The word has abstract meaning, ambiguous sense, a genuine word family, or a learner is struggling with retaining the spelling *after* clear linguistic instruction. Do not trigger simply because a learner has dyslexia.

1. **HEAR** the whole word in a contextual, spoken sentence. Check understanding; never require reading.
2. **UNDERSTAND MEANING** with an illustrative scene, simple explanation, real examples and learner choice of what the word means. For abstract words (e.g., `because`), show cause-and-effect scenes. Avoid implying a picture proves spelling knowledge.
3. **MAKE/BUILD**: learner creates or chooses a simple object, scene, diagram, personal association or visual arrangement that reflects meaning. Optional clay/off-screen physical modelling can be suggested where appropriate, without implementing branded Davis Symbol Mastery services.
4. **MAP** meaning and *verified* spelling: overlay true sound–grapheme patterns, orthography and morphology; explicitly teach what the picture alone cannot explain.
5. **RECALL INDEPENDENTLY** without the target spelled or a supplied list of exact letters. Permit full on-screen alphabet.
6. **REVISIT** on later dates for autonomous spelling and transfer to related words.

Record whether visual/meaning activities were used and whether they improved **delayed unaided spelling**, not merely engagement or three-choice recognition. Do not label a learner a fixed 'visual thinker' based on preference.

## 6. Evaluation plan

Run an initial specialist-reviewed pilot with word sets balanced by phonological/orthographic complexity and semantic concreteness.

- Baseline (common to both): educator-approved structured phonics/orthography/morphology lesson and accessible independent-spelling test.
- Variant A: baseline only.
- Variant B: baseline + optional learner-generated semantic/visual scene or manipulation.
- Variant C: baseline + equally timed alternative explicit explanation/practice (controls for extra time and attention).
- Outcomes: independently spelled words immediately, at 1 week and at 3–4 weeks; transfer to untaught words sharing the pattern, enjoyment, cognitive load and frustration.
- Analyse benefit by *word type and observed outcomes*, not presupposed visual learning-style group.
- Obtain child assent/guardian consent and privacy-preserving research design; do not use a speech model as a clinical pronunciation evaluator.

## 7. Product, copyright and licensing boundaries

Learn general principles from publicly described Davis research, but **do not** copy branded manuals, copyrighted exercises or proprietary training content. Davis®, Davis Symbol Mastery®, Davis Dyslexia Correction® and related programme designations are licensed trademarks. ## 8. Recommendation / research decision

**Explore a hybrid, not an equal blend of conflicting theories.** Retain explicit Structured Literacy and measurable delayed production as the non-negotiable core. Pilot meaning-first and tangible, child-created representations as supportive options informed by Davis, particularly for hard-to-imagine or abstract words, while rejecting unsupported universal explanations and untested claims of superiority.

## 11. Staging check and implementation status — 12 November 2026

The user asked whether recurring errors in the Year 6 staging game lead to a Davis-inspired alternative. **As of this check, they do not.** The currently deployed `year6.html` repeats a short written-correct-word reminder after a second incorrect attempt. It does not offer the optional meaning/visual-building route.

The new headless [optional meaning-making support policy](../core/meaning-making-policy.cjs) and [tests](../core/meaning-making-policy.test.cjs) are in GitHub. They encode an **opt-in choice** after at least two independent errors (or review errors): core-focused practice, meaning-and-making, or park for later. They require that the spelling, meaning and activity have been reviewed before the route can be enabled. It remains behind pedagogical approval gates and is **not yet wired into the playable staging game**. It must not impersonate branded/licensed Davis services.

**Research reaffirmed on 12 November 2026:** IDA's structured literacy framework explicitly includes hands-on, multimodal instruction. Galuschka et al. (2020) synthesize 34 controlled spelling trials, finding support for phonics, orthographic and morphological teaching. The published van Staden/Tolmie/Badenhorst (2009) Davis-based Afrikaans study used approximately 18 learners per arm and tested a multi-component intervention, not Spellmaster's proposed optional feature; its positive results are suggestive but do not demonstrate superiority or transfer to English. Therefore add Meaning & Making experimentally, with a **time-matched additional practice condition**, and track delayed *independent* spelling, rather than assuming it works.

Next UI acceptance criteria: second mistake offers a *brief spoken, optional* different route rather than a lengthy lecture; correct letters are shown during manipulation but hidden at unaided recall; meaning and association are relevant and accurate; learner can select ordinary quick help or park the word; no missed word produces a failure penalty; uptake and retained recall are tracked only with appropriate guardian consent for research.
