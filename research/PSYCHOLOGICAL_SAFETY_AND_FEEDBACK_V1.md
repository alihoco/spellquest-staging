# Spellmaster Psychological Safety and Feedback Standard v1.0

**Status:** Binding product design standard; research-informed and pending review by dyslexia educators and child-development specialists.
**Date:** 11 November 2026
**Primary outcome:** Accurate retained spelling skills, developed in a learner experience that protects self-esteem and encourages continued engagement.

## Founding rule

**Never judge a learner by a spelling attempt.** An incorrect answer is information about what to teach next, not a failure by the learner. Spellmaster must be emotionally safe for children and adults, but must not falsely label wrong spellings as correct.

## Evidence anchors

1. British Dyslexia Association, *Set Up for Somebody Else* (2025): in the surveyed young people, 70% reported dyslexia made them feel bad about themselves; 78% reported experiences of others assuming they were less clever. These are survey findings, not population prevalence estimates. https://www.bdadyslexia.org.uk/news/set-up-for-somebody-else
2. British Dyslexia Association, *Living with Dyslexia: Children and Young People's Experiences* (2025): repeated school difficulties, stigma, embarrassment, anxiety and avoidance are reported. https://cdn.bdadyslexia.org.uk/uploads/documents/Reports/BDA-Report_Set-up-for-somebody-else-report_Oct-2025.pdf
3. NHS, *Dyslexia in children*: dyslexia does not affect intelligence; positive experiences, support, enjoyment and confidence building are important. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dyslexia-in-children/
4. Education Endowment Foundation, *Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning* (2021): feedback must be actionable and task-focused; inappropriate feedback can impede progress and confidence. https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/feedback
5. EEF, *Feedback* toolkit: acknowledge correct work and give specific next-step guidance, not merely negative judgements or praise of innate ability. https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback
6. Scoping review (2025), *Challenging school-related situations faced by students with dyslexia*: negative school encounters, internalised shame and avoidance are documented. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11881-025-00356-9

**Evidence boundary:** These sources support a psychologically safe, accurate feedback design; they do not prove that eliminating all 'wrong answer' signals or providing constant praise improves retention. Our exact microcopy, rewards and frustration response need usability and outcome testing.

## Non-negotiable learner-facing policies

1. **No personal failure labels:** No "FAIL", "FAILED", "bad at spelling", "below your age", "stupid", punitive red X, negative sound, shame animation, public grade, peer rank or countdown punishment.
2. **Truthful information:** Do not present an incorrect spelling as correct. Privately distinguish "needs a different spelling pattern" and pinpoint an accurate next step.
3. **Specific, grounded feedback:** Describe exactly what worked and which part can be adjusted. Praise real retained knowledge, effortful strategy use, thoughtful correction and persistence when genuinely observed; avoid indiscriminate "genius" or "brilliant" labels.
4. **Choice and autonomy:** Learner can request a hint, replay audio, change input modality, skip/park a word or take a break without losing trophies or progress.
5. **Responsive after repeated difficulty:** After consecutive mistakes, reduce cognitive load, show a verified explanation, offer assisted building or switch to a reviewed alternative method. Do not demand endless retries.
6. **No forced reading or speaking:** Instructions available via verified narration and visible accessible text. Speaking aloud is optional; never use unreliable speech recognition to tell a child they said a word incorrectly.
7. **Age respect:** Never make an older learner see a babyish theme because of spelling gaps. Age and skill remain separate.
8. **No comparison:** No leaderboards, peer comparisons, or classroom norm scores in learner-facing UI.
9. **No loss aversion:** Do not confiscate earned trophies, streaks, coins or "Master" identity after missed days or imperfect retention; revisits become positive recovery adventures.
10. **Genuine success:** Mix previously learned words with new challenges on evidence-informed review schedules; celebrate correctly retrieved previous words, but never manipulate data to create an imaginary success.
11. **End sessions positively:** Offer a freely chosen finish, a reflection on something practised or learned, or a previously known word; do not force the last attempted word to be correct.
12. **Safety and dignity:** Avoid publicly displaying a learner's dyslexia status, detailed mistakes, vulnerability measures or emotional notes. Optional emotional self-checks must be minimal, non-diagnostic and not used to shame or manipulate.

## Feedback state machine

| State | User-visible narrative | Teaching action |
|---|---|---|
| First attempt accurate | "You remembered it without clues!" | Record independent response; schedule delayed recall |
| First attempt inaccurate | "Good first try. Let's explore the tricky part." | Identify teacher-verified mismatch; begin targeted teaching |
| Assisted completion | "You built the word using the clues. Now let's see what stays with you." | Keep assisted state separate; offer accessible unaided recall |
| Repeated difficulty | "This one's taking more practice. We can try another way." | Switch reviewed support or park word |
| Delayed recall successful | "You remembered this from another day — that's progress!" | Record genuine retained recall; mastery progress |
| Previously learned word forgotten | "Let's give this one a refresher." | Preserve earlier achievement; select smallest helpful reteaching |
| Learner chooses break | "You've practised today. Your adventure will be here when you return." | Persist progress without penalty |
| Teacher/word content cannot be verified | "Let's choose a different word for now." | Do not teach unverified sounds or invent feedback |

## Sample scripts (not fixed mandatory wording)

**Incorrect:** "You got the beginning right. The tricky part is the two S letters. Let's learn a way to remember them."
**Same error again:** "That part is still tricky. Shall we use letter tiles, or hear a different explanation?"
**Prior mastery revisited successfully:** "You learned this earlier, and you can still spell it today. That's something you've built over time."
**End of session:** "You explored three words and remembered one from last week. That's progress."

## Rewards and mastery

- Separate playful rewards for exploration/effort from educational mastery badges grounded in delayed independent recall.
- Personal growth across days, weeks, months and years is the only comparison shown to learners.
- All learners can attain "Master" within a skill domain, independent of age.
- No reward contingent on flawless performance, fast speed or uninterrupted daily streaks.
- Rewards never obscure that a word needs further practice; they never make difficulty a personal failing.

## What to measure

- Learning: independent initial accuracy, corrected accuracy after teaching, unaided retention after delay, pattern transfer.
- Experience: repeated voluntary engagement, fatigue, break/skips, self-selected assistance and opt-in learner confidence rating.
- Safeguarding: episodes of frustration, use of exit/help controls, feedback that learners describe as discouraging, accessibility barriers.
- Compare microcopy and experience designs using specialist-reviewed, appropriately safeguarded research before claiming benefit.

## Release gates

- Every feedback utterance reviewed for factual accuracy, warmth, dignity and specificity.
- No negative labels/punishments in learner interface or voice.
- Same psychological-safety rules for text, audio, animations, progress panels and game rewards.
- On erroneous learner input, factual correction plus an achievable next action must exist.
- No forced repetition or mandatory microphone evaluation; permission refusal does not block progress.
- Easy pause/skip, age-respectful theme and independent input options.
- Real learners including dyslexic children and adults provide feedback; accessibility and emotion checks pass.
- Explicit checks against made-up praise, false mastery and comparing people.
