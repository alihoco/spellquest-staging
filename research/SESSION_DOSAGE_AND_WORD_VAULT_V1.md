# Spellmaster short-session dosage, reminders and Word Vault policy — v1.0

Date: 12 November 2026
Status: research-informed product hypothesis, not an established optimal number of words/minutes.

## Evidence
- International Dyslexia Association: irregular words may need daily practice; only a few new words at a time, cumulative spelling patterns and supervised feedback. https://dyslexiaida.org/spelling/
- Petersen-Brown et al. (2023), 88 Grade 2–3 children: practice distributed across sessions improved 28-day spelling retention versus massed practice, with no immediate advantage. https://doi.org/10.1002/pits.22943
- Ruitenburg et al. (2026), 53 classroom studies, 7,303 learners: mean effects of retrieval (g=.37), spacing (g=.20) and interleaving (g=.40), with implementation/knowledge-dependent moderators; not dyslexia-specific dosage guidance. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-026-10225-5
- Veiga da Silva et al. (2023): delayed spelling benefit of retrieval plus feedback versus copying in one primary-school experiment. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37605436/
- Warwickshire literacy guidance: short frequent teaching interventions such as 5 minutes daily, illustrative practice guidance rather than proof of optimal exact duration. https://sasc.org.uk/media/gedbejuc/warwickshire-literacy-practice-guidance-oct-2020.pdf
- Klimovich & Richter (2025): German Grade 3 interleaving study, benefits qualified by prior knowledge; not evidence for a fixed English-dyslexia schedule. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12511507/
- Reward strategy review (2026): low-stakes and choice-supportive rewards have promising links to academic performance; does not validate specific goblin/stealing mechanic. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2026.100766

## Dosage hypothesis
- Start with **three new words per week as a ceiling/target, not a quota**, preserving school coordination where useful.
- Suggest **5–8 minutes per session, five days per week**, with a shorter option for fatigue, younger learners and challenging words. Duration is a hypothesis to test, not an age-based assessment.
- Begin each session with one or more due previously taught words; add at most one new word in a session and a provisional maximum of 3 per week. Many sessions contain 0 new words.
- Review under spaced retrieval with provisional checkpoints (e.g. next day, day 3, day 7, day 14, day 28), adapting by independent retention, word type and learner burden. **Do not** force word attempts on every checkpoint irrespective of user availability.
- Group a few related examples for first explicit teaching; mix review across patterns and prior words. Default two old/due words per one new word as a provisional ratio, adjustable after real retention data.
- Prioritise review backlog over the calendar target; never penalise missed days or overwhelm the child with accumulated missed reviews.
- If practice starts to produce frustration, stop or switch to a familiar legitimate review, not more new instruction.

## Daily reminders
- Parent/guardian-managed for minors; explicit opt-in, local time preference and quiet hours. Older learners/adults can manage their own reminders.
- Suggested text: 'Your five-minute Spellmaster adventure is ready when you are.'
- No streak-loss warnings, guilt, peer comparisons, repeated nagging or compulsory day-by-day engagement.
- Track whether reminders improve voluntary return and retained spelling, not just clicks. Allow pause/snooze/disable anytime.
- The product reminder capability is a future implementation requirement; **no reminder has been scheduled by writing this policy**.

## Word Vault / swag bag mechanic
Educational object lifecycle **NOT** a single high-stakes keep/lose flag:
1. **Discovered** — after hearing/being introduced to a target word, may be represented as a blank/locked treasure map.
2. **Collected** — after immediate independent correct spelling; stored in learner's bag as *in progress*, not called permanently mastered.
3. **Protected** — when a due delayed independent spelling is correct; earns a little reinforcement and further spaced reviews.
4. **Recovery quest** — when a due attempt is incorrect, the mischievous baddie hides/temporarily captures the word scroll. It is retrievable through supported teaching and later unaided recall; no earned trophies removed, no blaming words, no personal-failure fanfare.
5. **Mastered** — after educator-defined delayed independent successes and appropriate pattern transfer, earns significant mastery acknowledgement; the scroll always remains in the personal collection record even when a refresher is needed.
- Narration options for anxious/younger learners replace baddies with friendly puzzles or mysteries.
- A villain is defeated through **genuine retention**, not by completing a supplied-letter puzzle; helped spelling still makes progress, but does not award mastery.
- Rewards proportional: understated confirmation for an attempt, small coin/animation for authentic self-correction, a bigger celebration only for durable mastery.
- No irreversible loss, pay-to-win, public rankings, timer pressure or daily-streak sanctions.
- Behavioural safety pilot: ask participants how 'word theft' feels; if it triggers frustration, prefer 'the word needs rescuing' or an entirely noncombat theme.

## Pilot evaluation
Randomise/counterbalance comparable words and session structures as allowed by ethics review. Compare e.g. 2 vs 3 new words per week or shorter vs longer sessions only if feasible; control exposure and review frequency. Primary measure: 7-/28-day **independent unaided spelling**. Secondary: time to complete, dropout, fatigue, learner choice, confidence and game enjoyment. Do not extrapolate from one learner.

## Release constraints
Before building rewards UI, implement reliable persistence of word state, due dates, first-attempt correctness, assistance and mastery evidence. Separate *Word Vault story state* from *actual educational mastery state*. No claims of validated optimal dosage yet.
